# Originality and claim-boundary review

## Original contribution

- Uses the individual material claim—not the document—as the governed provenance object.
- Separates observed records, human assertions, deterministic derivations, model-generated interpretations and authoritative operating state.
- Detects recursive generated-source reuse, circular ancestry, shared-leaf false corroboration, semantic strengthening and scope widening.
- Introduces an ancestry fingerprint to distinguish document count from independent leaf evidence.
- Adds reverse descendant propagation for corrections without claiming that a changed record reverses prior action.
- Includes a new fictional Harbor Lattice example and a 55-field AI Evidence Ancestry and Recursion Gate.

## Boundaries

- Model output may remain useful information; it does not become observed facility fact through repetition, citation or wording review.
- Evidence class describes provenance, not guaranteed truth.
- Human review of summary fidelity is not presented as verification of the underlying physical condition.
- A citation to a generated artifact can be accurate while still incomplete for operating use.
- Different models consuming the same leaves do not create independent facility evidence.
- Ancestry depth is a trigger, not a universal quality score.
- Correction of a source does not reverse an executed action; outcomes and remediation require separate review.
- No real customer, facility, model, provider, deployment, incident, product availability or performance result is claimed.
- The fictional example is labeled before use and again in the disclosure.
- NIST AI 600-1 and AI RMF 1.0 are voluntary cross-sector resources. W3C PROV-O is a general provenance ontology. None validates this method or creates a self-storage requirement.
- The image is editorial and non-documentary; no unrecorded generation method or real meeting is asserted.
