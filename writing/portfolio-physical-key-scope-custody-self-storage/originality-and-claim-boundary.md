## Originality

- New operator problem: a visible ring labeled “master” without a maintained scope, copy count or current authority relationship.
- New practical tool: Portfolio Physical-Key Scope and Custody Register.
- New fictional example: Orchard Current Storage Group and a three-copy regional ring after a personnel transfer.
- No customer, deployment, adoption, loss, performance, revenue, occupancy, partnership, certification, award or recognition claim is made.

## Claim boundaries

- `Master` is treated as a local label, never as proof of scope.
- Possession, authorization, use, return, duplicate recovery, lock change and access termination remain separate states.
- A returned key proves only the return of the identified copy; it does not prove that no duplicate exists.
- A lock change changes a key-to-lock relationship only where supported by the service record and governed scope.
- Unknown scope and unknown copy count remain explicit exceptions.
- Detailed bitting, cut codes and reproduction-sensitive data are excluded from the ordinary operating register.
- The fictional example is labeled at entry and again in the disclosure.
- The article does not instruct staff to test unknown keys, enter restricted rooms, select locks, cut keys or change lock hardware.
- NIST SP 800-171 Revision 3 is scoped to CUI protection; NIST SP 800-53 is a federal security and privacy control catalog; the ISC/CISA document is federal-facility guidance. They are used as architecture references only.
- No NIST, CISA, ISC, self-storage-industry or legal compliance claim is made.
- The article is not legal, locksmith, insurance, personnel or security advice.
