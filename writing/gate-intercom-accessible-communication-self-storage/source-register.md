# Source register

Accessed November 25, 2026, America/New_York. Each published link was checked for its specific claim. The shift procedures and fictional scenario are editorial recommendations.

| Source and provenance | Use and limits |
| --- | --- |
| [DOJ guidance on effective communication](https://www.ada.gov/resources/effective-communication/) · U.S. Department of Justice Civil Rights Division | Communication methods and consultation, aids matched to the exchange and customer needs, treatment of relay calls and limits on asking companions to interpret. Federal guidance for covered entities. It neither assesses an individual site nor prescribes one channel for every interaction. |
| [DOJ Title III regulations, § 36.303](https://www.ada.gov/law-and-regs/regulations/title-iii-regulations/) · official U.S. Department of Justice regulatory text | Paragraph (c) addresses effectiveness, accessible formats, timeliness, privacy and independence. Paragraph (d)(4) addresses treatment of Title IV relay calls. Direct governing text for covered public accommodations. The article makes no facility-specific coverage, design-standard or compliance determination. |
| [FCC consumer guide to telecommunications relay service](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/GOVPUB-CC-PURL-gpo252945/pdf/GOVPUB-CC-PURL-gpo252945.pdf) · FCC Consumer and Governmental Affairs Bureau, hosted by the U.S. Government Publishing Office | Communications assistants and the limited scope of 711 dialing. General service guidance. It does not establish that a particular facility offers a specific service or that all relay types use 711. |
