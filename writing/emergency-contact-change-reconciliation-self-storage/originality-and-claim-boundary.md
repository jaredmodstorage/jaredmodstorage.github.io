# Originality and Claim-Boundary Review

## Original contribution

The article reframes emergency-contact maintenance as a governed multi-location change episode rather than a directory edit. It contributes a surface inventory, lifecycle states, safe-test boundary, privacy controls, facility-purpose reconciliation method, fictional six-facility example, operator card, and 40-field register.

The structure, prose, Keystone Harbor Storage example, identifiers, dataset, and tool were created for this package. No real employee departure, emergency, provider account, contact number, customer record, or operational result is used.

## Claim boundaries

- The article does not claim every facility uses every listed surface or should maintain identical contact sequences.
- It does not state that any named provider, system, municipality, responder, insurer, alarm account, or utility has a particular process.
- OSHA sources are used within workplace emergency-action-plan scope and are not represented as a universal self-storage contact-data specification.
- NIST CSF 2.0 is used only for a structural governance analogy concerning roles, communication, and third-party dependencies.
- Ready Business resources are planning aids, not regulations or certifications; direct retrieval limitations are disclosed.
- The article does not authorize emergency calls, responder dispatch, provider test mode, personal-phone distribution, employment action, access revocation, or account mutation.
- Keystone Harbor Storage, every facility, person, contact reference, provider, purpose, timestamp, request, test, row, and result are fictional.
- No deployment, customer, legal compliance, provider relationship, response time, performance, cost, revenue, occupancy, award, indexing, coverage, or recognition claim is made.
