Before You Hand Over a 10×10, Check the Route for the Largest Item

A move-in route check for self-storage managers, from the unloading point through the last opening.

By Jared Mastroianni10 min read

A customer can rent enough floor space and still be unable to use it. The office hears “10×10” and thinks about what will sit inside. The customer wants to know whether the largest piece can get from the truck to the unit. If the first time anyone checks is when the movers reach the door, the customer may already have paid for a truck and set aside half a day.

In a fictional move-in, a customer reserves an upstairs 10×10 for boxes and a rigid shop cabinet. The cabinet will arrive padded and must stay upright under the mover’s handling plan. The customer measures the wrapped piece at 41 inches wide, 37 inches deep and 73 inches high. The staff member measures 36 inches of clear width at the reserved unit’s doorway. Even with plenty of room inside, that doorway is too narrow for the upright cabinet. The customer needs an alternative before unloading.

I would start by asking which piece would be hardest to get in. That question tells the manager what to measure and gives the customer an answer they can use when the truck is still empty.

Ask what will actually come off the truck

Ask for the largest, longest or least flexible piece and find out how it will arrive. A customer may have a manufacturer’s width for a cabinet but be moving it on a base with corners wrapped. A sofa listed as two sections may be arriving in a single carton. Ask for the outside width, depth and height as it will travel, including fixed handles, wrapping and any moving equipment that adds to its dimensions on the route. Use the same units throughout the check.

Record who supplied the measurements and what they include. If the mover has a handling restriction, write it down in plain language. In the cabinet example, staying upright rules out a whole set of possible movements. Treat any different configuration as a new measurement and leave handling decisions to the people responsible for the move. Desk staff should never have to prove a fit by lifting or angling a customer’s property.

Check the reservation at the same time. A size category is a way to shop for storage. The actual unit and route are what the manager can measure. Public Storage’s 10×10 guide describes its dimensions as approximate and says that unit sizes may vary. That is a useful reminder when a reservation has yet to be assigned to a particular door.

If the reservation only holds a size, tell the customer when the unit number will be known. Put the route check on the same task as the assignment so it happens before the move-in instructions go out. If the office assigns a different unit afterward, reopen the check even when the new unit is the same size. This is especially important when one shift books the move and another assigns inventory after a unit is cleaned. An accurate measurement attached to the wrong unit is little help when the truck arrives.

Walk the route from the unloading point

Start where the customer is actually allowed to unload. Follow the same path they can use during their planned move-in hours. Check the usable width and height at entrances, any loading door, and the unit itself. At each tight turn, look at the clear area available on both sides of the corner. A hallway can be wide enough along each straight run while leaving too little room to bring a long piece around the bend. Measure to the surfaces the item will encounter, including fixed rails, guards, projecting hardware and overhead obstructions.

If the route uses an elevator, record its clear door opening and usable cab dimensions separately. Check room to approach, enter and unload on both floors. Confirm that the elevator is available for the planned use and that the mover can verify the intended load, including equipment and people, against the posted rating and site rules. Room & Board’s furniture delivery guide illustrates the difference between measuring an elevator doorway and measuring the cab. The same distinction matters on a storage move.

Finish at the actual unit. Measure the clear opening in its normal, fully open position, using the facility’s procedure for a staff member to check a vacant unit. Note any threshold or change in level, and check room to turn or place the item inside. Janus International’s self-storage door range includes different styles and opening sizes. A product specification can help identify the door. The field measurement describes what is available at this unit. If the door does not open or stay open normally, stop the check and put the issue through maintenance. Keep staff and customers clear of an unstable door.

Write the measurements in route order. A single minimum width is easy to read, but it cannot show where a restriction is, how high it is, or whether the item has room to turn. Include the exact unit, floor, loading entry, date and initials. A picture of a vacant opening can help the next shift recognize it if the facility allows photography. Use the measured values in the record and keep customer information out of those images.

Give the customer an answer they can act on

There are three useful outcomes. A known obstruction means a measured restriction rules out the stated configuration. Measurements available for review means staff can share what they measured and the customer or mover can assess the way the piece will travel. Fit unresolved means a measurement, route condition or handling detail still needs an answer. For close clearances or complicated turns, arrange a walk with the mover or keep the decision open. A few inches on paper do not account for hands, equipment, movement or measurement error.

In the fictional example, 37 inches is the smaller horizontal dimension of the rectangular upright cabinet, and the doorway gives 36. That is enough to rule out the reserved door under the stated handling plan. An available ground-floor 10×10 has a clear opening measured at 47 inches wide by 84 inches high and a straight approach. Staff can give those facts to the mover and ask for a review of the entire alternative route, including the entry and space beyond the threshold. It may solve the problem at the same nominal size. Quote the actual unit and terms, and obtain the customer’s agreement before changing the assignment.

If a customer is already on site, help them avoid a second failed attempt. Keep the item where it can remain safely under the mover’s control, away from shared routes and entries. Give the customer any verified alternative and explain who can decide a transfer or approve another option. When the mismatch came from a dimension the facility supplied, acknowledge it plainly and bring in the person who can resolve it. Passing the customer to another shift without the facts adds another wait to a move that is already off schedule.

Before the customer leaves the desk, repeat the decision in terms they can pass to the crew. Say which entrance to use, which unit is assigned and what the mover still needs to check. If the elevator or unloading area will be unavailable at the original appointment time, confirm the new time with the customer before anyone heads out. The job is finished at the desk when the customer knows what happens next and who will call if it changes.

Leave the next shift the measurements

Use the Route Card for Bulky Items for a bulky-item reservation before it becomes a day-of problem. Keep the item notes with the reservation. Keep measured openings with the facility’s unit and route records so future staff can find them without opening someone else’s rental file. A known route may serve several units, but the last opening still belongs to the specific unit. Mark measurements as due for a new check after changes to a door, railing, approach or access route.

A useful handoff tells the next employee which route was checked, what the customer knows and who is waiting on whom. If a mover still needs to confirm the turn at the elevator, record that one point and when someone will follow up. If an alternate unit is being considered, record its exact number and whether availability and terms have been confirmed. Staff should be able to continue the conversation without asking the customer to start from the beginning.

On the next shift, pick one of the routes that regularly takes bulky move-ins and walk it with a tape measure and the card. Compare what the office can tell a caller with what the caller will find at the first tight turn and the last door. Fix the gap in the reservation notes or unit record while the route is in front of you. The next customer should have a chance to choose a workable unit before the cabinet leaves the truck.

Route Card for Bulky Items

Use one card per assigned unit and route. Record measurements in a single unit of measure.

Field Record
Reservation Facility, date, move-in window, assigned unit, floor and route
Largest item Outside width, depth and height as moved, including packaging and added equipment
Handling Restrictions from the mover or customer, dimension source and measurement date
Contact Staff owner, next action and follow-up time

Walk in move-in order

  • ☐ Confirm the approved unloading location and that the planned route will be available.
  • ☐ Measure entrances, tight turns and usable space on both sides of each turn.
  • ☐ Record elevator opening and cab separately. Confirm site rules and who checks the intended load.
  • ☐ Check the assigned unit’s clear opening, threshold and placement space. Stop for a door that operates abnormally.
Route point Record
Unload and entry Location, operating hours, clear opening width and height
Hall and turns Narrow point, turn space on both sides and fixed projections
Elevator, if used Clear door width and height, usable interior width, depth and height, approach and exit
Assigned unit Unit number, clear opening width and height, threshold and room inside
Measurement record Date, initials, outstanding checks and changes that require remeasurement

Give the customer a status

Status Next action
Known obstruction Identify the measured conflict. Offer another route or unit for review when available.
Measurements available Share the exact measurements. Ask the customer or mover to assess the move and review any close clearance.
Fit unresolved Name the missing measurement or decision, who owns it and when they will follow up.

Fictional example. Upright, wrapped cabinet: 41 inches wide, 37 deep and 73 high. Unit door clear width: 36 inches. Status: known obstruction for the stated handling plan. An alternate unit’s opening measures 47 inches wide and 84 high. Share the dimensions and confirm the complete alternate route, availability and terms before changing the assignment.
